# Visual Style Guide

---

## Color Palette

| Color | Hex | Role | Usage Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ocean Blue** | `#0A2342` | Primary / grounding | Backgrounds, night scenes, title panels. Should cover 40–50% of any composition — this is the island's depth and the brand's foundation. Never used as a small accent; it's the canvas, not the detail. |
| **Curaçao Yellow** | `#FFD700` | Primary / identity | Chapter titles, key typography, hero highlights. Use for the single most important element in a frame — never more than one yellow focal point per image. |
| **Sunset Orange** | `#FF6B35` | Secondary / energy | Accents, underlines, warm lighting (golden hour), CTA elements. Signals momentum and warmth — use in transitions, action beats, and emotional high points, not static shots. |
| **Tech Cyan** | `#00D4FF` | Secondary / innovation | AI/tech motifs, circuit overlays, screen glows, futuristic elements. Reserve for anything representing systems, code, or "the build" — never for organic/natural elements (water, skin, wood). |
| **Coral White** | `#FFF5E6` | Neutral / breathing room | Body text on dark backgrounds, negative space, daylight scenes. Prevents the palette from feeling heavy — every composition needs at least one area of coral white to let the eye rest. |

**Ratio guideline per image:** ~45% Ocean Blue, ~20% Coral White, ~15% Curaçao Yellow, ~12% Sunset Orange, ~8% Tech Cyan. Tech-forward chapters (Spark, GPT Revolution, Software 3.0) may shift cyan up to 15–18%; heritage chapters (Boy from Curaçao, Family Legacy) may shift yellow and orange up instead.

## Typography System

| Use | Typeface | Weight/Style | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chapter titles** | Playfair Display | Bold, all-caps, +2px letter-spacing | Always Curaçao Yellow on Ocean Blue, or Ocean Blue on Coral White — never low-contrast pairings. |
| **Kickers ("Chapter One")** | Helvetica Neue | Uppercase, tracked wide, 12–14px | Always Tech Cyan — this is the only place cyan appears in text. |
| **Subtitles** | Playfair Display | Light Italic | Coral White, 18–20px. Reserved for the one-line emotional theme under each title. |
| **Body copy** | Helvetica Neue | Regular, 16–18px, 1.5 line height | Coral White on Ocean Blue, or Ocean Blue on Coral White. Never yellow or orange for long-form text — reserved for accents only. |
| **Pull quotes / signature phrases** | Playfair Display | Bold Italic | Sunset Orange, centered, generous whitespace above and below. |
| **Drop caps** | Playfair Display | Bold, 4 lines tall | Curaçao Yellow, paired with a small Tech Cyan circuit-line flourish at the base. |

## Imagery Rules

- **Cinematic first.** Every image should read like a still from a film, not a stock photo — implied motion, implied story, a moment caught mid-breath.
- **45-degree angles preferred** for portraits and character shots — avoids the flatness of straight-on framing while staying more grounded than an extreme dramatic tilt.
- **Natural light only** for human subjects — golden hour, blue hour, or soft overcast. No studio strobes, no harsh flash. Artificial light (screen glow, neon) is reserved for tech/interior moments and should always look motivated by an actual source in frame.
- **Drone and aerial shots** are reserved for scale moments — Kai Kòrsou, the island itself, "the whole block" thinking. Don't use aerials for intimate character beats; save the height for when the story earns it.
- **Water is a character, not a backdrop.** It should always be doing something — reflecting, moving, changing color — never a flat, static plane.
- **Human presence should feel caught, not posed.** Even portraits should imply the subject was doing something a second before the shutter.

## Texture Overlays

| Texture | Where to Use | Opacity |
|---|---|---|
| **Caribbean map lines** | Section dividers, chapter openers, background watermarks | 8–12%, Coral White or Tech Cyan on Ocean Blue |
| **Digital circuit overlays** | Any AI/tech/systems imagery, transitions between "roots" and "build" chapters | 15–20%, Tech Cyan, always fading at the edges — never a hard-edged grid |
| **Wave patterns** | Footers, chapter closers, transitions between chapters | 10%, Coral White, horizontal flow only — waves should always move left to right, mirroring the read direction |

Textures are connective tissue, not decoration — they should always signal "we are moving between ideas," never sit on top of a hero image competing for attention.

## Composition Principles

1. **Rule of thirds, always.** The horizon line sits on the lower or upper third — never dead-center — to keep every frame feeling like part of a larger story rather than a snapshot.
2. **Water as backdrop, land as foreground.** When both are present, the human subject and their action belong in the foreground on land or a dock; water fills the negative space behind them.
3. **Leading lines toward the horizon.** Bridges, seawalls, blueprint lines, and circuit overlays should visually point toward where the story is going, not just decorate where it is.
4. **One focal color per frame.** Even within the palette, pick a single dominant accent (yellow OR orange OR cyan) per image — layering all three competes for attention and breaks the cinematic stillness.
5. **Negative space is a feature.** Especially in title cards and quote graphics — Coral White or Ocean Blue open space gives the eye somewhere to land before it hits the type.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Do let golden hour and blue hour carry most of the emotional lighting.
- Do use tech cyan sparingly and only for genuinely tech/systems content.
- Do keep human subjects mid-action, mid-thought, or mid-conversation.
- Do use aerial/drone shots to represent scale and vision, not everyday moments.
- Do maintain the 45/20/15/12/8 color ratio as a baseline across the book.

**Don't:**
- Don't use harsh studio lighting or flat front-flash on any human subject.
- Don't mix more than one dominant accent color in a single frame.
- Don't use tech cyan on organic elements (skin, wood, water, plants).
- Don't center the horizon line — it flattens the drama.
- Don't let texture overlays exceed 20% opacity or gain hard edges — they should always feel atmospheric, never graphic.

## Sample Mood Board Descriptions

**"Roots" mood board (Chapters 1, 4, 9):** Warm dawn and dusk light, hand-painted colonial facades, weathered signage, family photographs with modern gradient treatment, wave-pattern textures, dominant Curaçao Yellow and Sunset Orange, minimal cyan.

**"Build" mood board (Chapters 2, 6, 7):** Blue-hour interiors, screen-glow lighting, circuit overlays at 15–20%, blueprint linework, dominant Ocean Blue and Tech Cyan, small accents of yellow for hope/breakthrough beats.

**"Scale" mood board (Chapters 5, 10, 12):** Aerial drone perspectives, wide horizons, layered cloud skies, full palette balance across all five colors, map-line textures at low opacity, the most "cinematic film poster" of the three boards.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

E stilo di e buki ta konta un solo historia ku kada koló: blou di laman pa profundidat, hel di Kòrsou pa identidat, orange di solpaseo pa energia, i cyan pa innovashon. Kada imagen mester tin composishon di siníma, luz natural, i solamente un koló dominante pa no distraé di e storia.
