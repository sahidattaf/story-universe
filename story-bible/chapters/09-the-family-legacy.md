## Narrative

Some children grow up with a family business in the background of every conversation. Mine was ATTAF REAL ESTATE. I didn't understand it as a company at first. I understood it as a language the adults spoke — land, lots, homes, who could be trusted, which deal to walk away from even when it looked good on paper. I learned the vocabulary of property long before I could explain what a deed actually did. I just knew that the word *land* was always said carefully in our house, the way you say the name of someone you respect.

In Curaçao, land is never abstract. The island is small enough that almost every piece of ground carries a story — a family that worked it, an elder who refused to sell it, a house that three generations called home. When your family's name is attached to land, it's attached to people's homes, their savings, their hopes for their children. You learn early that a name in real estate is not a logo. It's a promise that the ground under someone's feet was dealt with honestly.

*Land and a good name.* My family taught me those are the two things you protect first, and that both are measured in generations, not quarters. That lesson sounds simple until you watch it tested. A quick sale that would bend the truth. A buyer who wants you to look away from something. The discipline isn't in knowing the right answer. It's in choosing it when nobody would ever have found out.

For a long stretch of my life, I thought I'd walked away from all of that. I chose code over contracts, screens over lots, remote clients over neighbors who knew my family's reputation before they knew my face. I told myself I was building something different. Kai Kòrsou is what finally showed me I'd never left. The moment I stood on 120 hectares of raw coastline, every lesson from ATTAF REAL ESTATE came back — patience with land, respect for whoever stood on it first, the understanding that you are borrowing ground from the next generation, not consuming it.

The longer I looked, the more I saw the same inheritance hiding in the work I thought was separate. An AI system is a kind of property. People move into it. They trust it with their data, their time, sometimes their livelihood. Software 3.0, with its contracts, approvals, and rollback plans, is really a deed written for a new kind of land. A restaurant is a property people live inside for an evening. Everything I build, it turns out, is real estate — some of it just happens to be made of code and fire instead of concrete.

That realization brought me something I didn't expect: gratitude without the weight of obligation. I don't carry the family business as a debt I have to repay. I carry it as a foundation I get to build on. The people who carried the Attaf name through ATTAF REAL ESTATE before me did the slow, unglamorous work of making it mean *trustworthy* on this island. I walk into rooms today where that meaning arrives before I do. I didn't earn that. I inherited it. My job is not to spend it carelessly.

There's a particular quiet I feel when someone on this island hears my last name and their face softens a little — because of a house, a fair deal, a promise kept long before I was part of the story. That's what legacy actually looks like. Not a monument. A face softening at a name.

I don't wear the Attaf name like a crown. I carry it like a key to a house someone else built — and the only rule that comes with the key is to leave the house in better condition than I found it, for whoever turns the lock next.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Mi a lanta tende e idioma di ATTAF REAL ESTATE na mesa: tera, konfiansa, i un nòmber ku mester keda limpi.
Na Kòrsou tera no ta algu abstrakto — kada pida tera tin un historia, un famia i un speransa.
Mi no ta karga e nòmber Attaf manera un kroon, sino manera un yabi di un kas ku otronan a traha, pa lag'é mihó pa esnan ku ta bini.
