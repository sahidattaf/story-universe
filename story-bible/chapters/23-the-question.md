# Chapter 23 — The Question

The roof of my mother’s house is flat. It is made of concrete, painted a faded white. When I was a child, I used to come up here to hide. Now, I come up here to think.

It is 4:00 AM. The sky is still black, but the stars are fading. The trade winds are strong tonight. They smell like salt and dust.

I am sitting on a folded blanket. My laptop is next to me, closed. My yellow notebook is open on my lap.

The question is written at the top of the page: *What do I want to be building in ten years?*

I have been staring at it for three hours.

The offer from Silicon Valley is still in my inbox. Four hundred thousand dollars. I haven't opened the email again. I don't need to. The number is burned into my brain.

I think about the man in the hoodie at the conference. *It won't scale.*

I think about the recruiter. *We're moving fast.*

I think about Sahid on the seawall. *Who are you building for?*

I look at the question in the notebook.

And slowly, the answer starts to form.

I don't want to build a company. I don't want to build an empire. I don't want to build something that scales to a billion users.

I want to build a room.

A small room. A studio. A place with a desk and a chair and a window. A place where I can sit with three other people and look at a whiteboard. A place where we can build systems for the island. Small systems. Real systems. Systems that work.

I don't want to build for the world. I want to build for Curaçao. And maybe, if it works, for Aruba. And Bonaire. And Sint Maarten. But not for San Francisco.

I pick up my pen. I write below the question:

*I want to build a studio. 12 square meters. 3 people. One island.*

I look at the sentence. It is small. It is not impressive. It is not "venture-scale."

But it is mine.

---

The door to the roof opens. My mother comes out. She is wearing her robe. She has two cups of coffee.

"You've been up here all night," she says.

"I couldn't sleep."

She sits down next to me. She hands me a cup.

"You're going to take the job," she says. It is not a question.

"No," I say.

She looks at me. Her eyes are tired but sharp.

"Why not?"

I point to the notebook. She reads the question. Then she reads the answer.

She is quiet for a long time.

"That's a small studio," she says.

"Yes."

"You could do more."

"I could. But I don't want to."

She looks at me. Then she smiles. It is a small smile. The kind of smile that says *I understand*.

"Good," she says. "Small is fine. Small is how everything starts."

---

At 6:00 AM, I open my laptop. I find the email from the recruiter.

I press "Delete."

I open a new tab. I book a flight to Aruba.

Not to Silicon Valley. To Aruba. To Yolanda Croes. To the fishermen's cooperative. To the first client of my new studio.

I close my laptop. I look at the sky. The sun is coming up. The clouds are turning gold.

I stand up. I pick up my yellow notebook.

I go downstairs. I make breakfast for my mother. We eat together.

Then I pack a bag. One small bag. A laptop, a notebook, three shirts.

I walk out the door.
