## Narrative

The water in Willemstad doesn't just sit there — it performs. Every morning it turns a different color before it settles, like it's rehearsing for something bigger than the day. I used to sit on the seawall by Punda before school, watching the pastel houses across the Sint Annabaai wake up pink and blue and gold, and I'd think: *this place is already a dream. Somebody just has to build the rest of it.*

I was that somebody, even before I knew it.

My name is Sahid Jonathan Attaf, and I come from an island most of the world reduces to a postcard — turquoise water, floating market, a bridge that swings open to let the big ships through. But postcards don't show you the part that matters. They don't show you my grandfather's name still stitched into real estate contracts across this island, the Attaf name meaning something before I ever earned a single thing myself. They don't show you a kid doing homework by the light of a secondhand computer, teaching himself code between blackouts, because something in him already understood that the next empire wouldn't be built with concrete first — it would be built with ideas, compiled at 2 a.m., in a bedroom that smelled like salt air and printer ink.

Curaçao raises you two ways at once. It teaches you patience — island time is real, and you learn to let things ripen. And it teaches you hunger — because you watch talent leave for Amsterdam, Miami, Houston, chasing rooms this island doesn't have yet. I felt that pull too. But somewhere along the way, I stopped asking *how do I leave this island* and started asking a better question: *how do I build the room here myself?*

That question is the seed of everything that came after it — the AI tools, the restaurant, the 120 hectares of coastline I'm not just dreaming about but drafting into reality. But none of it starts in a boardroom. It starts on this seawall, with a boy who was told the horizon was the edge of what's possible, and who decided, quietly, stubbornly, that the horizon was just the first blueprint line.

My family didn't hand me a company. They handed me something better — a name tied to land, to trust, to a way of doing business where your word outlives your paperwork. Sevier Attaf built houses for people to live in. I'm building systems for people to grow in. Different tools, same inheritance: the belief that this island deserves world-class things designed *by* the people who grew up smelling its rain before it falls.

I am not from Curaçao in the way a tourist is *at* Curaçao. I am *of* it — the Papiamentu in my mouth before English ever arrived, the Dutch discipline layered on top like a second skeleton, the ocean as my first teacher of patience and my first proof that something enormous can move without ever seeming to hurry.

This chapter isn't about where I've arrived. It's about the ground I arrived *from* — hand-painted rooftops, a family name older than my ambition, and a boy on a seawall deciding, without permission from anyone, that he was going to build something the horizon hadn't seen yet.

The dream didn't start in Silicon Valley. It started here. It was always going to start here.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Kòrsou no ta un kuadro pa turista — e ta e suela mará na mi nòmber, e agua ku a siña mi pasenshi promé ku ambishon. Fam'ia Attaf a laga mi un erensia di tera i konfiansa, i awor mi ta traha ku AI i visionan nobo riba e mesun fundeshi. E soño no a kuminsá na Silicon Valley — e a kuminsá aki, riba e muraya di laman na Punda.
