## Narrative

At 06:30 every morning — before email, before the grill order, before any client has a chance to rearrange my priorities — I stand in front of the dream board. People assume it's decoration, a collage of nice pictures to feel good about. It isn't. It's the closest thing I have to an instrument panel, and I read it the way a pilot reads instruments: not to admire it, but to check whether I'm still on course.

The idea didn't start with me. Kevin Trudeau talks about dream building — not a vision board that collects dust, but one you look at daily, feel, and act on — and about the question underneath it: *what would you do if you knew you could not fail?* The first time I answered that honestly, the list embarrassed me. It was too big for the island, too big for my bank account, too big for the version of me still underpricing invoices. That embarrassment was the point. It showed me exactly where my ceiling was, and that I'd built it myself.

So the board grew. A photo of the seawall at Punda, top left, where everything started. A sketch of long tables by the water that eventually became BOSSA. An aerial of raw coastline with blueprint lines drawn over it — Kai Kòrsou, still more drawing than building. Icons for every GPT I've shipped. The eight gates of Software 3.0. And across the bottom, seven words I'll explain in the next chapter. Half of it is printed and pinned. Half of it lives on a screen beside it, updated as things move. Paper for what I'm committed to. Pixels for what's still changing.

Trudeau has an image I think about often: set an autopilot one notch off, and a plane headed for Boca Raton lands hundreds of miles away. Small distinctions decide everything. That's what the board catches. Not the big failures — those announce themselves. The small drift. The week I said yes to three things that weren't on the board and quietly starved the one that was. At 06:30, the board doesn't judge me. It just shows me the gap, and the gap is information.

But I want to be precise about something, because precision is respect: a dream board without action is wallpaper. The board never built anything. It only told me what to build next, and then the day had to do the work — the code at dawn, the fire at dusk, the site walks at golden hour. Every item that has moved from *someday* to *in progress* moved because someone picked up a tool, not because someone stared at a picture long enough.

The turn came when I stopped keeping it private. On camera, for *Dream Board Live*, I showed the whole thing — including the items that are still just lines on a map and the ones running behind schedule. I expected to feel exposed. Instead, people started answering with boards of their own: a first menu, a list of scholarships, a photograph of an empty lot with *someday* written across it in marker. That's when I understood what I'd been doing all these years. The ritual wasn't superstition. It was a method — and a method is something you can hand to someone else.

That was the moment the dreamer and the builder stopped being two people sharing one body. The boy on the seawall who imagined things and the man with the spreadsheet who delivers them had always been reading the same board. They just hadn't looked up at the same time.

Now, at 06:30, I still stand there with my coffee. The board is more crowded. The gaps are smaller. And somewhere across this island, other people are standing in front of theirs, reading their own instruments, choosing their own next move. That's the one thing I never pinned to the board — because I never dared to dream it.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Tur mainta na 06:30 mi ta lesa mi dream board manera un piloto ta lesa su instrumentonan.
E no ta un lista di deseo; e ta un sentro di komando kaminda soño ta bira plan, i plan ta bira akshon.
Ora otro hende a kuminsá traha nan mesun board, mi a komprondé ku e soñadó i e konstruktor ta un solo hende.
