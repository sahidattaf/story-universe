# Chapter 3 — The 7-Year Build

---

## Narrative

Nobody claps for year one. Nobody claps for year three, either, or year five, if I'm honest about it. The seven years between "I can write code" and "people will pay me to think for their business" were mostly invisible — not because I was hiding, but because there simply wasn't an audience yet for work that hadn't proven itself. I learned early that credibility isn't announced. It's assembled, quietly, one delivered project at a time, usually while nobody outside the room is watching.

I started the way most people start on an island with more talent than infrastructure: doing work that was beneath what I knew I could eventually do, because it was the work that was actually available. Small business websites. A spreadsheet automation here, a scraped-together database there. Clients who didn't fully understand what I was building for them, which meant I had to learn to explain complexity in plain Papiamentu and plainer English before I ever learned to charge properly for it. I underpriced myself constantly in those years. Every consultant on this island has a version of that story — the invoice you're embarrassed to send, the scope that quietly tripled while the fee stayed the same, because you were more afraid of losing the relationship than protecting your own time.

What kept me building through that was a very specific kind of stubbornness — not the loud kind, the quiet kind. Kevin Trudeau has a phrase I didn't hear until much later but recognized instantly when I did: *the wealthy don't wait for the perfect moment.* I wasn't waiting for a perfect moment in those seven years. I was manufacturing small ones — a client testimonial here, a tool that actually saved someone real hours there, a referral that came not because I'd marketed myself but because the work had simply held up under use. Credibility, I learned, compounds exactly like code does. Nothing is wasted. Every function you write once, you never fully write again — you just call it, improved, in the next project.

There were nights I doubted the whole premise. Curaçao is a small market, and AI consulting in the early years looked, to most people around me, like a hobby dressed up as a business. I'd sit with the numbers — inconsistent income, clients who ghosted mid-project, competitors in bigger markets who had resources I didn't — and ask myself the honest question: *is this actually working, or am I just stubborn enough not to notice that it isn't?* The answer, most of those nights, was unclear. I kept going anyway, because the alternative — closing the laptop for good — felt more permanent than any uncertainty the work itself could produce.

The breakthrough, when it finally arrived, didn't look like a single dramatic moment. It looked like the fourth or fifth time a client came back on their own, unprompted, with a bigger problem and a bigger budget, because the first small thing I'd built for them had simply kept working long after I'd been paid and moved on. That's when I understood what those seven quiet years had actually been buying: not visibility, but trust that didn't need to be re-earned every time. GPT Innovation by Attaf didn't begin as a bold declaration. It began as the accumulated weight of dozens of unglamorous deliveries finally becoming heavy enough to stand on.

I think about that seawall from Chapter One often during this stretch of the story — the boy deciding the horizon was a blueprint line. What I didn't yet understand at seven years old was that blueprints take years to become buildings, and most of those years look like nothing at all from the outside. They look like a man at a desk, alone, refining a proposal nobody asked for yet, betting that the version of himself on the other side of year seven would thank the version of himself grinding through year three.

He does. Every day.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Shete aña no tabata un kaminda di aplauso — tabata un kaminda di trabou ketu ku niun hende no tabata mira. Kada fakturashon barata, kada duda meianochi, tabata un blòki den e fundeshi. Ora e konfiansa finalmente a bini, e no tabata un momento dramátiko — e tabata e peso akumulá di tantísimo trabou chikí bira sufisiente pesu pa para riba dje.
