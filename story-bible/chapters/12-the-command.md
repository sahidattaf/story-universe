## Narrative

The water in Willemstad is still performing. That hasn't changed. It still turns a different color every morning before it settles, still rehearses for something bigger than the day. I'm back on the seawall at Punda — same stretch of stone, same view of the pastel houses waking up across the Sint Annabaai. The boy who sat here before school would recognize everything in front of him. What he wouldn't recognize is the man sitting in his spot.

Behind me, the city is starting its day, and somewhere in it the grill at BOSSA will be lit this afternoon for people I haven't met yet. On the laptop beside me, tools I built are already answering questions in three languages, in time zones where it's still night. Past the curve of the coast, 120 hectares are waiting — more blueprint than building, still, but drawn now, and drawn carefully. And in a command center that lives mostly in code, a set of gates is holding my operation steady, one approval at a time. Not everything around me is finished. But it's real, and once, none of it was.

There's a phrase that runs underneath this whole book: *your wish is your command.* For a long time I misunderstood it. I thought it meant that wishing hard enough makes the universe obey. It doesn't. The command isn't something you give the universe. It's something you give yourself — every morning, at 06:30, in front of a board that tells you the truth. The wish sets the direction. The command is the discipline to walk it when nobody is clapping. That's the only magic I've ever seen work.

So before anything else, gratitude. To this island, for teaching me patience with its tides and hunger with its departures. To my family and ATTAF REAL ESTATE, for handing me land and a good name and trusting me to leave both better. To the regulars at BOSSA, who reminded me that people remember tables, not dashboards. To the clients who came back in year three, when nothing about me looked like a sure thing. To everyone who watched a video and built a board of their own. And to the boy on this seawall, who decided — without permission — that the horizon was the first blueprint line. He was right. I'm still drawing it.

I thought finishing this book would feel like arriving. It doesn't. It feels like the moment after you've poured a foundation and stepped back to look at it: quiet, solid, and a little impatient, because now you can finally see what goes on top. The seven words don't end at "I am." They loop. Every "I am" becomes the next "I wish," just one level higher.

Island time taught me patience. Deadlines taught me hunger. The seawall taught me that you can stay exactly where you started and still travel further than anyone who left. I'm not from Curaçao. I'm of it. And the island isn't finished either — it's waiting for more people to sit on these stones and decide the same thing I did.

The sun is fully up now. The water has settled into its daytime blue. The bridge will swing open soon for a ship I'll never board, and I'll watch it go without the old pull in my chest. I have somewhere to be. There's code to review, a grill to check, a coastline to walk at golden hour, and a board waiting for tomorrow's 06:30.

This is the last chapter of this book. It is not the last chapter.

*Kiko nos ta traha awe?* What are we building today?

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Mi a bolbe na e mesun muraya di laman na Punda, i e isla rònt di mi a kambia — algu ya konstruí, algu ainda blueprint.
"Bo deseo ta bo komando" no ta majia; e ta disiplina, gratitud i akshon tur dia.
E buki aki ta kaba, pero e historia no: kiko nos ta traha awe?
