# Chapter 13 — The 120 Hectares

One hundred and twenty hectares.

Say it out loud. Let it sit in the mouth like a piece of green mango — sweet, sharp, a little dangerous if you bite too fast.

One hundred and twenty hectares of coastline on the western edge of Curaçao. Land the sun touches first in the morning and last at night. Land that has been watched by the same lighthouse for over a century, and by the same stars for longer than that.

This is Kai Kòrsou. This is the project that took me from software to soil.

People ask me why. They look at my life — the AI systems, the restaurant, the consulting — and they say, "Sahid, you built digital things. Why would you go buy land?"

I tell them the truth: I didn't go buy land. The land came and found me.

There is a moment every Curaçaoan knows. You are standing on a cliff at Westpunt, or walking the salt flats at Jan Kok, or driving the road to Boka Samí at six in the morning when the light does that bronze thing it does — and you feel it. A pull. Not nostalgia exactly. Not patriotism exactly. Something older. Something that says: *this is yours, and you are responsible for it.*

I felt that pull for years and ignored it. I had software to build. Chapters to write. Threads to spin up.

Then one afternoon, my father called me. He said, "Come look at something."

We drove out past the salt pans. The land opened up — nothing but limestone, divi-divi trees, and the sea on the horizon like a rumor. He did not say anything for a long time. Then he said, "This was almost sold last year. To a foreign developer. They wanted to put up a wall and sell the view."

He paused.

"I made sure that didn't happen."

That was the moment. Not a pitch. Not a spreadsheet. A father standing on his island, looking at land that had almost been taken from the family before the family even knew it could be theirs.

My father built ATTAF REAL ESTATE from nothing. He learned the deed system the way I learned the codebase — slowly, then suddenly, then absolutely. He spent his life moving pieces of this island between hands so that the right people ended up holding them. He never called it a legacy. He called it a Tuesday.

But standing on that land with him, I understood something I had been avoiding: *software runs on servers. Legacy runs on land.*

So we bought it. Not all at once — that is not how these things work. Piece by piece, deed by deed, negotiation by negotiation. 120 hectares assembled like a codebase, one commit at a time.

And now? Now it is Kai Kòrsou.

The vision is simple to say and enormous to build: a village that feels like the island, not like a resort that happens to be on the island. Homes that breathe. A harbor where local boats and visiting boats share the same water. A hotel that does not wall off the view but opens it. Restaurants — BOSSA among them — where the menu changes because the fisherman's catch did. Trails that connect the ridge to the beach. And around all of it, 40 hectares left wild, so the iguanas and the flamingos and the divi-divi trees get to keep their version of the story.

In the old model, a project this size required an army. A decade of consultants. A shipping container of permits. A wall of financing so tall you could not see the island behind it.

In the new model, it looks like this: a Notion page. A set of agents. A coordinator thread. A daily 6 a.m. sync. Me, on the seawall at Boka Samí, dictating decisions into my phone while the sun comes up.

The island does not need to import its developers anymore. The island does not need to import its capital, its consultants, or its ideas. The island can compose them.

One hundred and twenty hectares is not a number I say casually.

I say it the way my father said the word *deed* — as something you are borrowing from the next generation, not something you fully own.

Kai Kòrsou means "House of Curaçao."

It is not a house I am building for myself.

It is the house I am building so that the next kid from the island — the one who does not yet know he is an architect, an entrepreneur, a father of projects — has somewhere to come home to.

## Papiamentu Summary

**Capítulo 13 — E 120 Hectare**

Shen binti hectare. Bis'é na bos altu.

Esaki ta e número ku a kambia mi bida. No ta un número di software. Ta un número di tera.

Mi tata a traha ATTAF REAL ESTATE for di nada. E tabata move pida di e isla aki entre mannan, asina ku e hende korekto tabata sostené nan. Nunka e no a yam'é un legado. E tabata yam'é un djamars.

Un dia e a yama mi. "Bin mira algu." Nos a sosegá pa e tera west. E tera a habri — nada ma piedra di kalki, palu di divi-divi, i laman riba horizonte manera un rumor.

E tera aki tabata kasi bendí na un desaroyadó stranhero. Nan tabata ke traha un muraya i bende e bista.

Mi tata a hasi sigur ku esei no a sosodé.

Esei tabata e momento. No un pitch. No un spreadsheet. Un tata pará riba su isla, mirando tera ku kasi a wordu tuma for di e famia promé ku e famia a sa ku e por tabata di nan.

Awor nos tin 120 hectare. Awor e ta Kai Kòrsou — "Kas di Kòrsou."

E vishon: un pueblo ku ta sinti manera e isla, no manera un resort ku a kai aki. Kasnan ku ta respira. Un haf kaminda botnan lokal i botnan di bishita ta kompartí e mesun awa. Un hotel ku no ta sera e bista sino habri'é. I rònt di tur kos, 40 hectare laga salvehe, pa iguananan, flamingonan, i palu di divi-divi por keda ku nan bersion di e historia.

Den e modelo bieu, un proyecto asina grandi tabata rekerí un ehérmito. Dies aña di konsultantenan. Un container di permit. Un muraya di finansamentu asina haltu ku bo no por mira e isla tras di dje.

Den e modelo nobo, e ta parse asina: un página di Notion. Un set di agente. Un coordinator thread. Un sync di 6 a.m. tur dia. Mi, riba e seawall na Boka Samí, dikta decisionnan den mi telefon mientras solo ta sali.

Kai Kòrsou no ta un kas ku mi ta traha pa mi mes.

Ta e kas ku mi ta traha pa e mucha di e isla — esun ku no sa ainda ku e ta un arkitekto, un entrepreneur, un tata di proyecto — tin un lugá pa yega kas.
