# Chapter 2 — The Spark

---

## Narrative

The computer wasn't new. It wasn't even mine, not really — it belonged to the household, a secondhand tower somebody's cousin had brought back from Curaçao's endless network of who-knows-somebody, humming under a desk in the corner of a room that also held the ironing board and a stack of my school books. But the first night I turned it on and watched the screen flicker awake in the dark, something in me went quiet in the way it only goes quiet right before it goes loud.

Willemstad has blackouts. Everyone here knows the particular darkness of a grid that gives out mid-evening, the whole street holding its breath by candlelight until the hum of the refrigerators returns. I learned to love those blackouts, strange as that sounds, because they taught me to work by the light of whatever still had a charge — a laptop battery, a phone screen, my own stubbornness. When the power came back, I didn't run outside to celebrate with the rest of the block. I sat back down at the keyboard, because I'd found something that didn't need permission from the grid, the weather, or anyone's schedule but mine.

I didn't understand code as "code" at first. I understood it as a door. Type the right words in the right order, in a language that looked like nonsense to everyone else in the house, and the machine would do something it hadn't done a second before. A window would open. A shape would move. A page would load that hadn't existed until I told it to exist. Nobody on this island had handed me that kind of power before — not because they were withholding it, but because nobody around me yet knew it was there to hand over. I found the door myself, at night, with the ironing board for company.

I taught myself in fragments — a tutorial here, a forum thread there, half of it in English I was still stretching to fit, the rest pieced together the way you piece together a recipe from watching your grandmother instead of reading it off a card. Papiamentu got me through frustration. English got me through documentation. And somewhere in the middle, a third language started forming — the syntax itself, brackets and semicolons, a grammar that didn't care what accent you carried, only whether the logic was sound. That felt like the most honest fairness I'd encountered up to that point in my life. The machine didn't care where you were from. It only cared if you were right.

I broke things constantly. I'd write forty lines and get one symbol wrong and the whole thing would collapse, and I'd stare at the error like it was a locked gate instead of a note left for me to read carefully. That was the actual spark — not the first time something worked, but the first time something failed and I didn't feel defeated by it. I felt *informed*. The computer wasn't rejecting me. It was teaching me exactly where to look next. That is a different relationship with failure than most of the island grows up with, where a closed door often means the door is simply closed. Here, a closed door came with a manual, if you were patient enough to read it.

By the time the sun came up on those early nights, I hadn't built anything the world would call impressive. A broken little script. A page that loaded slow and ugly. But I had built the belief that I could keep doing this — that this glowing rectangle in a corner of a cluttered room was a legitimate place to spend a childhood's curiosity, and maybe, eventually, a life's work.

I didn't know yet about AI tools, about consultancies, about a restaurant or 120 hectares of coastline. I only knew that the machine had opened, and that I wanted to keep walking through whatever door it showed me next. Looking back, that's the whole story in miniature — not a genius moment, just a boy who found something honest, in the dark, and decided to stay.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Un computadó di mondo di kas a habri un porta pa mi ku niun otro kos no por a habri. Den skuridat di un blackout, mi a siña ku e idioma di kódigo no ta wòri di unda bo ta bini — solamente si bo lógika ta korekto. Ei mi a haña e promé fe di ku mi por konstruí algu ku propio mannan.
