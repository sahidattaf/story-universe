## Narrative

For years, I believed the work should speak for itself. It's a very island belief — you don't announce, you deliver, and if what you built is good, people will find it the way they find a good fish stand: by word of mouth, by smoke on the wind. I built AI tools people used on other continents and a restaurant where a stranger once told me the fish tasted like his grandmother's, and I explained almost none of it to anyone. I called that humility. Some of it was. Some of it, if I'm honest, was fear wearing humility's shirt.

The first time I pointed a camera at myself, I recorded more takes of a thirty-second clip than I want to admit, and deleted every one. The problem wasn't the lens. It was what the lens asked of me: stand here, say what you believe, and let people disagree with you in public. Code doesn't argue in the comments. A grill doesn't screenshot you at your worst angle. For a man who trusts what he can touch more than what he can only see on a screen, visibility felt like building on sand.

What changed my mind was a question I kept hearing from young people on this island, in slightly different words each time: *how did you do this from here?* Underneath it sat a quieter assumption — that to matter, you have to leave. Every time I heard it, I understood something uncomfortable. My silence wasn't neutral. It was a missing example. Somewhere on this island there's a kid on a seawall who needs proof that the room can be built here, and proof that stays hidden inside a laptop isn't proof to anyone but me.

So I stopped hiding the work. I mapped it into pillars — *Island AI*, *Build in Public*, *The Architect's Desk*, *Curaçao to the World*, *Dream Board Live* — not as a marketing plan, but as a promise. Show the work. Show the parts that don't work yet. Explain AI to a fisherman and a fisherman's instincts to an investor in the same tone, because nobody on either side deserves to be talked down to.

Visibility came with a price nobody puts in the thumbnail: responsibility. When you become a face for something, every sentence carries more weight. Once I say out loud that this island can build world-class things, I had better keep building them — in public, where the delays are visible too. *Mi palabra ta mi kontrato* — my word is my contract. That was true when my word only reached a client across a table. It's heavier when it reaches screens I'll never see.

Then there's the vulnerability of the numbers. A video I believed in underperforms. A comment misreads everything I meant. I've learned to give those moments an hour — feel it, take what there is to learn, then move on — because vanity metrics are loud and short-lived. What I remember for weeks is the message from someone who says a video finally made AI feel like something meant for people like them. Legacy outranks likes. It always will.

I've also had to accept that being the face isn't the same as being the whole story. Behind every clip are the people who keep the fire going at BOSSA, the clients who trusted me in year three, the island that taught me everything I'm now explaining back to it. The camera points at me, but the credit belongs to a much wider frame.

I don't think I became a face of island innovation because I'm the best at it. I became it because someone had to go first, and the camera was already sitting on the desk. Every video I publish is my way of standing on the seawall in public — so the next boy or girl watching the harbor wake up pink and gold doesn't have to wonder whether it's possible. They can just watch it happen, and start.

## Papiamentu — Resumen

Pa hopi aña mi a laga e trabou papia so, pero mi silensio no tabata humildat — e tabata un ehèmpel ku tabata falta.
Awor mi ta mustra e proseso, inkluso e partinan ku no ta bai bon ainda, pa e hóbennan di isla mira ku nan no mester bai pa nan konta.
Mi palabra ta mi kontrato: ora bo ta e kara, kada palabra ta pisá mas.
