1. FULL NARRATIVE
The Island Protocol

The video call starts at seven in the morning, Curaçao time.

Which means it is also seven in Aruba, seven in Bonaire, and — depending on the season — seven or eight in Sint Maarten. The Caribbean is one time zone stretched across four hundred miles of water.

Four faces. Four islands. One question.

The face on the top-left screen belongs to Yolanda Croes. She runs the Aruba Tourism Authority's digital program. She has been in the room with the big consultants — Deloitte, McKinsey, the firms that fly in, present a slide deck the size of a small hotel, and leave. She is tired of it in a way I recognize.

"Sahid," she says. "We saw what you built in Curaçao. The Command Center. The agents. The chapters." She pauses. "We want you to install the same thing for us."

There is a moment when a sentence like that lands, and I feel my old self standing up to answer it. My old self would have said yes. My old self would have started talking scope and timeline and retainer. My old self would have taken the contract.

I do not answer as my old self.

"No," I say.

The call goes quiet. On the bottom-right screen, a man from Bonaire — Marcel, I think — shifts in his chair.

"No," I repeat, "but not because I don't want to help. Because install is the wrong verb."

Yolanda's eyebrows go up. She is not offended. She is curious.

"Installing means I bring Curaçao's version and drop it on your island. That works for three months. Then the scope drifts. Then the support bills pile up. Then the next consultant arrives with a better slide deck. That is the old model. That is exactly what we are trying to escape."

"So what is the new model?" she asks.

"A protocol," I say. "Not a product. Not a service. A blueprint you can hold, fork, and rebuild with your own hands."

I spend the next ninety minutes walking them through it.

Not a demo. A diagram. I open my screen-share and I show them the four layers.

One — the folder. Every island gets one. It is named after the island. It is owned by the island. It lives on the island's own cloud.

Two — the coordinator. One prompt. The prompt says: read these files, delegate these threads, review these outputs. The prompt is public. Anyone can read it. Anyone can improve it.

Three — the agents. Narrative writer. Image prompt generator. Video script converter. Notion sync. Analytics. Monetization. The same six threads we run in Curaçao. Each one configurable. Each one optional. Each one replaceable.

Four — the cadence. Every island runs its own rhythm. Curaçao syncs at six in the morning. Aruba wants seven. Bonaire wants noon. Sint Maarten wants an evening pass because of the time zone. The protocol does not care. It is a heartbeat, not a clock.

"That's it?" Marcel asks from Bonaire.

"That's it," I say. "No licenses. No proprietary anything. It is public on GitHub tonight."

"And what do you want in return?" Yolanda asks.

I think about it for a moment. What do I want?

"I want four folders," I say. "Not one. Four. If I build one Caribbean system, it is mine. If we build four, it is ours. The point is not to be the man with the system. The point is to be one of the men and women with the system."

Yolanda smiles. Marcel writes something down.

The folder goes up that night.

The name is simple: Caribbean Protocol — 4 Islands, 1 Folder.

Inside, there are four subfolders:

Curaçao

Aruba

Bonaire

Sint Maarten

Each one has the same skeleton. Empty pages waiting for content. Empty agent prompts waiting for island-specific instructions. Empty cadences waiting for the heartbeat of that island's working day.

The next morning, at six o'clock Curaçao time, the first thread wakes up.

It is not mine. It is Yolanda's — she forked the folder overnight and started writing her own version of the coordinator prompt. At 6:02, a second thread appears from Bonaire. At 6:15, Sint Maarten. At 6:45, Curaçao.

Four threads. Four islands. One morning. One folder.

The island chain has become an archipelago of agents.

I close the laptop and walk to the seawall.

The sun is doing that bronze thing it does. The boats are starting to move. Somewhere on the horizon, an island I cannot see is waking up too.

I think about what a protocol really is. Not a rulebook. Not a contract. A protocol is a handshake between strangers who agree to trust the same shape.

I think about my father, who spent forty years moving pieces of this island between hands. He never called it a network. He called it Tuesday.

The Caribbean does not need to be saved. It is not drowning. It is not broken. It has never needed a consultant to explain it to itself.

The Caribbean needs to be composed.

And the composition has already started.

4. PAPIAMENTU SUMMARY
Capítulo 15 — E Protokolo di Isla

E video call ta kuminsá na shete di mainta, ora di Kòrsou.

Cuatro kara. Cuatro isla. Un pregunta.

Yolanda Croes, di Aruba, ta puntra mi si mi por "instalá" e sistema di Kòrsou na Aruba.

Mi ta bisa nò.

Nò pasobra mi no ke yuda. Nò pasobra instalá ta e verbo robes.

"Instalá" ta nifiká: mi ta bring e vishon di Kòrsou i mi ta laga'é riba bo isla. Esei ta traha pa tres luna. Despues e scope ta drift. Despues e siguiente konsultant ta yega ku un slide deck mihó. Esei ta e modelo bieu.

E modelo nobo ta un protokolo. No un produkto. No un servisio. Un blueprint ku bo por tene, fork, i rekonstruí ku bo propio mannan.

Mi ta mustra nan e cuatro nivel:

Un — E folder. Kada isla tin su mes. Nombrá pa e isla. Propiedat di e isla. Riba e nub di e isla.

Dos — E coordinator. Un prompt. Públiko. Kualke hende por lesa'é. Kualke hende por mehor'é.

Tres — E agente nan. Seis thread. Meskos ku Kòrsou. Kada un konfigurabel. Kada un opshonal.

Cuatro — E cadencia. Kada isla tin su propio ritmo. Kòrsou ta sync na 6 a.m. Aruba na 7. Bonaire na merdia. Sint Maarten na anochi.

E folder ta subi e mesun anochi: Caribbean Protocol — 4 Islands, 1 Folder.

E siguiente mainta na 6:00, e promé thread ta lanta. Nò ta di mi. Ta di Yolanda. Na 6:02, Bonaire. Na 6:15, Sint Maarten. Na 6:45, Kòrsou.

Cuatro thread. Cuatro isla. Un mainta. Un folder.

E kadena di isla a bira un archipiélago di agente.

Karibe no mester wordu salba. E no ta sink. E no ta kibra. Nunka el a mester un konsultant pa splika e mes.

Karibe mester wordu komponé.

I e komposishon a kuminsá kaba.
