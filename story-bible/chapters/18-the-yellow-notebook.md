# Chapter 18 — The Yellow Notebook

The steps in front of the house in Suffisant are painted blue. They have been painted blue for as long as I can remember. Every year, my mother repaints them because the sun eats the color, and every year she says the same thing: "A house that doesn't look cared for will stop caring for itself."

I am sitting on the third step. The bottom step is broken. It has been broken since I was twelve.

I have a yellow notebook open on my lap.

It is my third one. My mother gave me the first one when I was fifteen. She had bought it from a stationery shop in Punda. It was the cheapest one on the shelf, but it was bright yellow, and to me, it looked like a piece of the sun. She said, "Write down your ideas. If you write them down, they are real."

I am writing down an idea right now.

I am twenty-two years old. I just finished an interview at a company called GPT Innovation. The man who interviewed me — Sahid Attaf — asked me one question that I cannot stop thinking about.

He said, "What do you want to build?"

He did not ask me what I wanted to be. He did not ask me where I saw myself in five years. He asked me what I wanted to build.

I told him the truth. I said, "I want to build a system that keeps my mother's clinic from losing patients."

My mother works at a small clinic in Suffisant. She works double shifts. She comes home exhausted. And the clinic is always losing patients — not because they don't care, but because they forget. They forget their appointments. They forget their medication. They forget to come back.

I told him I wanted to fix that. I wanted to build a small system that sends them reminders. Simple. Cheap. Real.

He looked at me for a long time. Then he wrote something down in his own notebook. He said, "Come back tomorrow."

That is why I am sitting on the steps.

I am holding the yellow notebook, and I am trying to decide if I should go.

Because going means leaving my mother. Going means walking into a building on Scharlooweg, into a room with a whiteboard and three other people, and pretending that I belong there. I am twenty-two. I have a cracked laptop and a notebook. I am nobody from Suffisant.

My mother comes out of the house. She is wearing her clinic uniform. She is holding a cup of coffee.

She sits down next to me on the third step.

"You're going," she says.

It is not a question.

"I don't know if I'm ready," I say.

She looks at me. She has the same eyes as my grandmother. Tired, but steady.

"Bo no ta bai pa bo sa," she says. "Bo ta bai pa bo siña." (You don't go because you know. You go because you will learn.)

She stands up. She kisses the top of my head. She walks down the broken steps and gets into her car.

I look at the yellow notebook.

I write one line: *I am going to build it.*

I close the notebook. I stand up. I walk toward Scharlooweg.

---

The office is on the second floor. It has tall windows and a wooden floor. It smells like coffee and new paint. There are three chairs. There is a whiteboard. There is a man in a linen shirt standing in front of the whiteboard.

He is drawing something. It looks like a diagram of threads.

I stand in the doorway. I feel the way I felt on my first day of school.

He turns around. He sees me. He does not smile — he is not a man who smiles easily — but his eyes change. They soften a little.

"Melissa," he says.

I walk in. I sit in the middle chair.

He writes one word on the whiteboard. Big letters. **ORCHESTRATE.**

"This is what you're going to learn," he says. "Not how to code a product. How to conduct a system."

I take out my yellow notebook. I open it to a fresh page. I uncap my pen.

He starts talking. And I start writing.

Mi mama ta sali paden, bisti su uniform di klinika.

"Bo ta bai," e ta bisa. No ta un pregunta.

"Mi no sa si mi ta kla," mi ta kontestá.

Mi ta habri mi kuaderno hel i mi ta skirbi un liña: *"Mi ta bai traha'é."*

Mi ta drenta e oficina. E hòmber ta pará dilanti di e whiteboard. E ta skirbi un palabra grandi:

**ORCHESTRATE.**

No ta kódigo. Ta kondusí un sistema. Mi ta habri mi kuaderno, mi ta tuma mi pen, i mi ta kuminsá skirbi.
